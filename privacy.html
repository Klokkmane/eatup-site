EatUp

Privacy Policy

Effective October 25, 2026

EatUp is made by Kyle Lokker ("we"). This policy explains what EatUp collects, why, and the choices you have. In short: we only collect what the app needs to work, we never sell your data, and there are no ads or trackers.

What we collect

  • Your account: your email address, your name if you sign in with Apple or Google and choose to share it (or type it in), and your answers to "What brings you to EatUp?" if you give them.
  • Your kitchen: the food in your fridge (names, amounts, expiration dates, where it's kept), your grocery list, your recipe ideas, and the expiration dates you correct (so the app learns how long food lasts for you). They are backed up and available on your other phones.
  • Receipt photos: when you scan a receipt, the photo is sent to our server to read the items on it. We don't keep the photo after it has been read.
  • Barcodes and products: when you scan a barcode or pick a product from the food list, the barcode number or product is sent to our server to look it up.
  • Usage counts: how many receipts and barcodes you have scanned and when you last got new recipe ideas, to apply the limits of the free plan.
  • Your plan: whether you have Premium and until when. We never see your payment details; Apple handles payment.

Reminders are scheduled on your phone; we don't send or see them. If you use the microphone to say what you bought, your speech is turned into text by Apple's speech recognition, and we only receive the text. EatUp does not collect your location, contacts, or advertising identifiers.

How we use it

Only to run EatUp: to sign you in, save and sync your fridge and grocery list, read your receipts, suggest recipes, and apply your plan. We don't sell or rent your information, and we don't use it for advertising.

Services that help run EatUp

A few trusted services process data for us, only to provide the app:

  • Supabase stores your account, fridge and grocery list, and runs our server.
  • Anthropic (Claude) reads receipt photos and writes recipe ideas. It receives the photo or the list of foods in your fridge, not your name or email.
  • Unsplash and Replicate provide recipe pictures. They only receive the name of a dish.
  • Open Food Facts (a free, public product database) looks up scanned barcodes. It only receives the barcode number.
  • Apple and Google handle signing in if you choose them. Apple handles purchases and payment.
  • RevenueCat keeps track of your Premium subscription. It receives a random account code and your purchase details from Apple, not your name or email.

Keeping and deleting your data

We keep your data while you have an account. You can delete your account at any time in the app (Settings → Delete account), which permanently removes your account and everything saved with it from our servers. Food saved on your phone stays there until you delete the app.

Security

Data is sent over encrypted connections, and each account can only read its own data. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your information.

Children

EatUp is not meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their information.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see, correct, or delete your data. You can delete it yourself in the app, or email us for anything else.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top and, for important changes, let you know in the app.
